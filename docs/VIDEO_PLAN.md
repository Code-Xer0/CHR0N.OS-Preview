# CHRON.OS Video Plan

The first CHRON.OS videos should make the public beta feel real without overexposing the internal stack. Use synthetic demo archives only. Do not show private files, credentials, real personal archives, or unreleased source surfaces.

## Production Rules

- Use synthetic demo data and the public v0.2.2 beta lane.
- Caption every video.
- Export 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1 variants where practical.
- Keep claims to public beta reality: local intake, provenance, search, context, aging signals, and safe workflows.
- Treat MNEM.OS, Nest, Cerberus, policy automation, retention, and environment memory as staged direction, not shipped public features.
- Say the safety line plainly: CHRON.OS copies and indexes before it mutates; destructive workflows require explicit policy or operator approval.

## Video 1: Public Beta Tour

Length: 90 seconds

Purpose: Show that CHRON.OS is a real public beta download lane for local-first archival intelligence.

Structure:

1. Open with the public site and installer lane.
2. Show local source browsing and a working archive.
3. Show provenance: source path, hashes, metadata, and sidecars.
4. Show search-oriented organization, chronology, duplicates, and dashboard telemetry.
5. Close with the boundary: public beta now, deeper interop staged.

Script:

> CHRON.OS is local-first archival intelligence for files that need memory. The public beta focuses on capture, provenance, search, context, and safe archive workflows. It copies important files into a working archive without mutating originals, preserves where they came from, and helps surface what needs review. Advanced automation, interop, cloud polish, and full multimodal inference are staged until the trust gates are ready.

## Video 2: Safety Workflow

Length: 60 seconds

Purpose: Make file automation feel safe by showing the non-destructive model.

Structure:

1. Show messy folder or demo source set.
2. Show import into CHRON.OS as a copy workflow.
3. Show source path and original file untouched.
4. Show archive telemetry and aging/review signals.
5. End on explicit policy gating.

Script:

> CHRON.OS starts with custody, not automation. Originals stay where they are. The public beta copies, indexes, and preserves provenance before any policy action is considered. Delete, move, retention, and automation flows should be gated by explicit policy or operator approval. That is the trust model: inspect first, decide second, automate only when the rules are clear.

## Video 3: Archive Policy Wedge

Length: 75 seconds

Purpose: Explain who CHRON.OS is for without sounding like a generic sales pitch.

Structure:

1. Individuals: messy downloads, old documents, scattered projects.
2. Teams: provenance, aging files, archive rules.
3. Organizations: archive layer inside a broader local-first Hyperion deployment.
4. Boundary: public build focuses on archival intelligence.

Script:

> For individuals, CHRON.OS helps recover scattered files and forgotten archives. For teams, it preserves provenance, reviews aging files, and prepares policy-based retention workflows. For organizations, CHRON.OS can become the archive layer inside a broader local-first Hyperion deployment. The public beta is focused on archival intelligence; the deeper automation and interop stack remains staged.

## Video 4: Ecosystem Teaser

Length: 30 seconds

Purpose: Name the deeper direction without promising it is shipped.

Structure:

1. CHRON.OS as archive memory.
2. MNEM.OS continuity bridge as staged direction.
3. Nest automation dispatch as staged direction.
4. Cerberus telemetry proposals as staged direction.
5. Return to public beta download lane.

Script:

> CHRON.OS is the public archive layer of a larger local-first intelligence stack. Archive memory comes first: capture, provenance, search, context, and review. Continuity bridges, automation dispatch, telemetry proposals, and policy retention are staged behind trust gates. Start with the public beta. Let the archive remember before the system acts.
